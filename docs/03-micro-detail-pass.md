## GLOBAL CONTEXT (re-paste at the top of every section)

```
This is the micro-detail pass for Blackroot Basin. The macro world (terrain,
roads, buildings, biome layout) already exists. We are now adding surface-level
detail: ground cover, materials, weathering, clutter, particles, and decals.

THE DETAIL BUDGET PRINCIPLE:
Detail is tiered by distance from camera, and every tier has a hard budget.
- Tier 0 (0-12 m):   full micro-detail. Individual grass blades, pebbles,
                     debris, decals, surface imperfections.
- Tier 1 (12-45 m):  reduced. Grass in clumps, pebbles as merged scatter,
                     decals at half resolution.
- Tier 2 (45-140 m): meso only. No ground clutter. Simplified props.
- Tier 3 (140 m+):   macro only. Billboards and terrain.
Detail NEVER pops. Every tier transition is a dither-fade or scale-fade across
at least 15% of the tier's distance range.

NOTHING IS PLACED BY HAND AT THIS SCALE.
Every micro-detail element is generated by a rule: a density function, a
scatter algorithm, a weathering formula, or a procedural texture recipe. When
I specify "1,400 pebbles per hectare," that is a density parameter, not a list.
Write the generator, expose the parameter in config.js, and seed it
deterministically so the world is identical every load.

ALL TEXTURES ARE GENERATED IN-ENGINE.
No downloaded images. Every texture is drawn to an OffscreenCanvas at load
time using the recipes in Section I, then uploaded as a THREE.CanvasTexture.
Cache generated textures in an atlas; never generate the same texture twice.

PERFORMANCE IS A HARD CONSTRAINT, NOT A GOAL.
If a detail feature cannot hold 60 FPS at its specified density, reduce the
density parameter and report the reduction. Do not silently drop the feature.
Every section must report: added draw calls, added triangles at Tier 0, added
texture memory, and frame time delta.
```

# SECTION I — Procedural Texture Engine

Everything visual downstream depends on this. Build it first.

## I.1 The noise toolkit

Implement in `src/gfx/noise.js`, all operating on a 2D canvas ImageData:

| Function | Signature | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `valueNoise2D` | (x, y, freq, seed) | Base randomness |
| `perlin2D` | (x, y, freq, seed) | Smooth gradients |
| `simplex2D` | (x, y, freq, seed) | Faster, fewer artifacts |
| `fbm` | (noiseFn, octaves, lacunarity, persistence) | Layered detail |
| `ridged` | (fbm result) → 1 − abs(n) | Rock strata, veins |
| `worley2D` | (x, y, cellSize, seed, distMode) | Cells, cracks, pebbles, scales |
| `voronoiEdges` | (worley F2 − F1) | Cracks, mortar lines, tile grout |
| `domainWarp` | (noiseFn, warpStrength, warpFreq) | Organic distortion — critical for making rust, moss, and dirt look natural rather than noisy |
| `directionalBlur` | (imageData, angle, length) | Brushed metal, water streaks |
| `gradientRamp` | (value, colorStops[]) | Map noise to a palette |
| `posterize` | (value, steps) | Stratified rock, cartoon banding |

**Every texture in the game uses `domainWarp` at least once.** Un-warped fbm reads as "computer noise" instantly and is the single biggest tell of procedural texturing.

## I.2 Texture output format

Every material generates a **4-map set** at the resolutions below, packed into a shared atlas:

| Map | Resolution | Channels | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Albedo | 512×512 | RGB | Base color |
| Normal | 512×512 | RGB | Derived from a height field via Sobel, strength per material |
| Roughness+AO+Height | 256×256 | R=rough, G=AO, B=height | Packed, one texture fetch |
| Detail (tiling) | 128×128 | RGB normal | Tiled at 8× the base UV for close-up micro-surface — this is what makes surfaces hold up at 0.5 m viewing distance |

Generate at **two scales** for every ground material: a macro map tiled at 1/12 m and a micro map tiled at 1/1.5 m, blended in the shader by camera distance. Single-scale tiling produces visible repetition at exactly the distance players spend most of their time looking.

## I.3 Texture recipes

Each recipe is a step list. Implement literally.

**Asphalt** (`#3A3B3E` base)
1. Fill base `#3A3B3E`.
2. Worley F1, cellSize 3 px, mapped to ±9% brightness → aggregate stones.
3. fbm(simplex, 4 oct, freq 0.06), ±5% brightness, domain-warped by strength 8.
4. voronoiEdges, cellSize 90 px, threshold 0.04, darken by 22% → cracks. Apply a 2 px blur then a second pass at threshold 0.015 for hairline cracks.
5. Random dark patches: 6 blobs of fbm-masked `#2E2F32` at 35% opacity → tar repairs.
6. Random light streaks along one axis at 8% opacity → tire polish.
7. Roughness: 0.82 base, −0.15 in the tire polish streaks (worn asphalt is smoother).
8. Normal strength 0.7. Detail map: fine aggregate at cellSize 2 px.

**Concrete** (`#87857F`)
1. Base fill, then fbm(perlin, 5 oct, freq 0.02) ±7% brightness, warped by 12.
2. Worley cellSize 1.5 px at 4% opacity → sand aggregate.
3. Form-tie holes: 4 circles, radius 3 px, darkened 30%, on a regular grid offset per instance.
4. Horizontal form lines every 96 px, 1 px, darkened 12%.
5. Streak staining downward from top edge: vertical gradient noise, `#6A6862`, opacity ramping 0 → 25% over the top 40%.
6. Efflorescence: 3 pale blobs `#B4B0A6` at 18% opacity near the bottom edge.
7. Roughness 0.88. Normal strength 0.45.

**Brick** (`#7A4A3C` with `#C4BFB4` mortar)
1. Draw a running-bond grid: brick 190 × 57 px equivalent, mortar 10 px.
2. Per-brick hue jitter: ±8% in hue, ±14% in brightness, ±6% saturation. **Every brick must be individually varied** — uniform bricks are the classic failure.
3. Per-brick fbm surface at freq 0.15, ±5%.
4. Mortar: fill `#C4BFB4`, add worley cellSize 2 px for sand, recess by 3 px in the height map.
5. Chip 6% of bricks: erase a worley-shaped corner, reveal a darker `#5E382D` interior.
6. Weathering hook: expose a `mossMask` and `stainMask` output for Section L.
7. Roughness 0.9 brick, 0.95 mortar. Normal strength 1.0.

**Wood plank** (`#6B5943`)
1. Plank width 84 px, gap 3 px darkened 60%.
2. Per plank: 1D fbm stretched 20:1 along the grain, ±11% brightness → grain lines.
3. Knots: 0–2 per plank, concentric ellipse rings via a radial distance field warped by 6, darkened 30%.
4. Per-plank base hue jitter ±9%.
5. Nail heads: 2 per plank end, 3 px circles, `#4A4744`, with a rust bleed halo `#7A4A32` at 20% opacity for the weathered variant.
6. Edge wear: lighten plank edges by 12% over 4 px.
7. Roughness 0.85 (0.62 for the painted/varnished variant). Normal strength 0.85.

**Corrugated metal** (`#8C8A85`)
1. Sine height profile, period 76 px, amplitude 6 px, along one axis.
2. Rust: domain-warped fbm mask (strength 20, freq 0.03), thresholded at 0.55, filled with a 3-stop rust ramp `#8A4A28` → `#6B3418` → `#3E2410`. Rust concentrates at the corrugation troughs (multiply mask by the inverse height) and at panel edges.
3. Fastener rows every 76 px along the ridges: 4 px circles with a rust bleed streak running downward 24 px.
4. Dent noise: 5 low-frequency height dimples per panel.
5. Roughness 0.55 clean, 0.92 rusted, lerped by the rust mask. Metalness 0.85 clean, 0.05 rusted.

**Glass**
Transmissive `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, transmission 0.92, roughness 0.04, thickness 0.02, IOR 1.52, tint `#C8D8DC` at 6%. Add a subtle smudge map: 4 domain-warped fbm blobs at 5% roughness increase. Broken variant: a `voronoiEdges` crack network radiating from a strike point, with 8–14 missing shards cut from the geometry.

**Grass ground** (`#6B7F43`)
1. Base with fbm(simplex, 6 oct, freq 0.04) ±14% brightness, warped 14.
2. Second fbm at freq 0.008 for macro patchiness ±10%.
3. Dead patches: 5 worley-masked regions, `#8A8B52`, 40% opacity.
4. Dirt showing through: worley cellSize 12 px, thresholded at 0.85, `#6E5A42`.
5. Micro detail map: fine cross-hatched directional noise simulating flattened blades.
6. Roughness 0.95. Normal strength 0.35 (ground normals should be subtle — the grass geometry does the visual work).

**Mud** (`#4A3F30`), **Sand** (`#C2B49A`), **Snow** (`#E8ECF0`), **Rock stratified** (`#5D5B58`), **Scree** (`#9A958C`), **Peat** (`#33351F`), **Roof shingle** (`#4A4744`), **Vinyl siding** (`#C9C2B2`), **Rusted steel** (`#7A4A32`), **Painted drywall** (`#D8D4CA`), **Carpet** (`#7A6E60`), **Vinyl floor tile** (`#B4AFA4`), **Ceiling tile** (`#DEDAD2`), **Chain link**, **Cinder block**, **Tarmac line paint**, **Water surface normal**, **Cracked earth**, **Moss** (`#4A6B32`), **Lichen** (`#9AA88C`), **Ice**, **Gravel**, **Railroad ballast**, **Cargo container paint** (6 colorways), **Farm crop rows**, **Cemetery granite**.

Write each of these using the same step-recipe structure. Each gets: base fill, at least 2 noise layers with domain warping, at least one structural feature (grid, strata, cells), at least one weathering layer, per-instance jitter, and packed roughness/AO/height.

## I.4 Per-instance variation

Every material instance in the world gets a **per-instance seed** driving:
- Hue rotation ±6°, saturation ±10%, brightness ±8%.
- UV offset (random) and UV rotation (0/90/180/270 for tileable materials).
- A weathering intensity value 0–1 (Section L).

This is done in the shader via instance attributes, not by generating unique textures. 118 houses must not share one identical siding texture.

**HOW TO TEST:** Every material renders at 0.5 m viewing distance without visible pixelation or obvious tiling. Standing at 40 m, no repeating pattern is identifiable. Two adjacent brick buildings look related but not identical. Total texture memory under 220 MB.

# SECTION J — The Grass and Ground Cover System

The literal blades. This is a single GPU-instanced system serving every biome.

## J.1 Blade geometry

Each grass blade is a **7-vertex, 5-triangle** tapered strip:
- Base width 0.011 m, tapering to 0.0 at the tip.
- 4 height segments, so the blade can curve.
- Curvature: the blade bends along its length by a per-blade `bendAmount` (0.15–0.55 rad total), with the bend concentrated in the upper 60%.
- Per-blade random rotation around Y (0–2π), random lean direction, and a random tilt from vertical of 5–25°.
- Normals face outward from the blade's curve, with a slight outward flare at the edges (2-sided rendering, `DoubleSide`, with normal flipping in the shader for the back face — otherwise backlit grass goes black).

**Vertex colors** along the blade: darker at the base (×0.62 brightness), brightest at 70% height, slightly desaturated and yellowed at the tip. This gradient is what reads as "grass" more than the texture does.

## J.3 Density and distribution

Density is computed **per-square-meter from a function**, not from a fixed count:

```
density(x, z) = baseDensity[biome]
              * slopeFactor        // 1.0 at 0°, linearly to 0.0 at 40°
              * moistureFactor     // from distance to water + biome
              * shadeFactor        // 0.7 under dense canopy, 1.15 at forest edge
              * trafficMask        // 0.0 on roads, 0.15 within 1.5m of road edge,
                                   //    0.3 on paths, 0.0 under building footprints
              * fbm(x, z, 0.03)    // 0.55–1.45 patchiness — grass is never uniform
              * fbm(x, z, 0.4)     // 0.8–1.2 fine clumping
```

Base densities (blades per m²):

| Biome / zone | Blades/m² |
|---|---|
| Suburban lawn (mowed) | 2,400 |
| Suburban unmowed / verge | 1,600 |
| Open meadow | 1,100 |
| Farm field edge | 900 |
| Forest floor (mountain) | 320 |
| Alpine above Y 110 | 180 |
| Swamp margin sedge | 700 |
| Standing water reeds | 420 |
| City planter / park | 2,000 |
| Cracks in pavement | 40 (weed species only, along crack splines) |
| Scree / snow / mud | 0 |

At 2,400 blades/m² across a 12 m radius Tier 0 disc, that's roughly **1.08 million blades**. This is achievable at 60 FPS as a single `InstancedMesh` with a 5-triangle blade — **5.4M triangles is over budget**, so:

- Tier 0 (0–12 m): full density, 5-triangle blades.
- Tier 1 (12–28 m): density ×0.45, 3-triangle blades (drop 2 height segments).
- Tier 2 (28–45 m): density ×0.15, 1-quad cross-billboard clumps representing ~12 blades each.
- Tier 3 (45 m+): no geometry. The terrain's grass texture and normal map carry it.

Resulting Tier 0 triangle cost: budget **1.6M triangles maximum** for grass. Tune `baseDensity` down globally if exceeded, and report the multiplier applied.

## J.4 The scatter algorithm

Do not scatter randomly per frame. Use a **deterministic tile system**:
- The world is divided into 4 m × 4 m grass tiles.
- Each tile's blade positions are generated once from a hash of `(tileX, tileZ, seed)` using stratified jittered sampling (a 32×32 sub-grid with per-cell jitter), giving even coverage without visible grid alignment.
- Tiles within the Tier 0 radius are uploaded to a ring buffer of instance attribute arrays. As the camera moves, only tiles entering or leaving the radius are rewritten — never rebuild the whole buffer.
- Each blade's instance data: `position (3), rotationY (1), scale (1), speciesIndex (1), colorJitter (1), bendPhase (1)` = 8 floats.

## J.5 Wind

A single global wind system driving grass, foliage, cloth, particles, and water. In `config.js` under `WORLD.WIND`:

```
baseDirection: 215°   (matches the sun azimuth — wind and light should agree)
baseSpeed: 3.2 m/s
gustAmplitude: 4.5 m/s
gustFrequency: 0.08 Hz
gustNoiseScale: 0.012   (spatial — gusts travel as visible waves across a field)
turbulence: 0.35
```

In the grass vertex shader, blade displacement:
```
windPhase = dot(worldPos.xz, windDir) * gustNoiseScale + time * gustSpeed
gust      = fbm(windPhase) * gustAmplitude + baseSpeed
sway      = sin(time * 1.7 + bendPhase) * turbulence
bend      = (gust + sway) * heightAlongBlade^2 * bendStiffness[species]
```
Bend is applied as a rotation around the blade base, **quadratic in height along the blade**, so the base stays planted and the tip whips. Linear bending looks like the blade is sliding.

**Gusts must be visible as travelling waves** across an open field — that spatial noise term is what sells the whole system.

**Player interaction:** grass within 1.2 m of any character capsule bends away from it, falling off over 0.4 s after they pass. Sample character positions into a small texture (16 nearest characters) read by the grass shader. Sprinting characters bend grass 1.6× harder and further.

## K.2 Surface response requirements

Every impact must produce, simultaneously:
1. A **decal** at the hit point, oriented to the surface normal, offset 0.002 m to avoid z-fighting, with per-material size variance ±30% and random rotation.
2. A **particle burst** of 8–24 particles, colored per the table, with velocity biased along the surface normal ± a 40° cone, gravity-affected, lifetime 0.6–1.4 s.
3. A **surface-specific sound** at the impact position, with a distinct near (< 25 m) and far (> 25 m) variant.
4. For `thin` and `pass-through` materials, an **exit effect** on the far side.
5. For `GLASS`, immediate **geometry replacement** with a shattered variant plus 14–22 falling shard rigid bodies that persist 8 s.

## K.3 Footstep audio matrix

Every material needs **4 sample variants** (procedurally generated, varied by filter and envelope) at 3 intensities: walk, run, land. Land is 1.8× the volume and adds a low-frequency thump. Water depth modifies any footstep with a splash layer. Audible ranges per Phase 2 rules, modified: `GRAVEL`, `SNOW`, `CORRUGATED`, and `WOOD_PLANK` are audible at **1.35×** normal range. `CARPET`, `GRASS`, `DIRT`, and `MUD` at **0.75×**. Players should learn to route over quiet surfaces.

**HOW TO TEST:** Shooting each of the 40 materials produces a visually and audibly distinct result. Walking across a lawn onto a driveway onto a wooden porch produces three clearly different footstep sounds. Drywall penetrates, brick doesn't, glass shatters completely.

# SECTION L — Weathering, Aging, and Grime

This is the system that separates "assembled from primitives" from "a real place." It runs as a shader layer over every material.

## L.1 The weathering shader layers

Applied in this order in every fragment shader, each with a per-instance intensity 0–1:

**Layer 1 — Ambient dirt accumulation.** Darken by up to 18% based on `1.0 - saturate(worldNormal.y)`, i.e. upward-facing surfaces collect dirt. Modulated by a domain-warped fbm at world-space frequency 0.4 so it isn't uniform. Strongest in the city and industrial zones, weakest in alpine.

**Layer 2 — Water streaking.** On vertical surfaces, apply downward streaks originating from horizontal edges, window sills, ledges, and pipe outlets. Generate as a 1D noise stretched vertically 40:1, thresholded, with intensity decaying over 2.5 m of fall. Color: darken 20% and shift toward `#5A564E`. **This single effect does more for realism on concrete buildings than any other.**

**Layer 3 — Edge wear.** Detect geometric edges via a curvature term (screen-space derivative of the normal, or a baked vertex AO/convexity attribute). On convex edges, lighten and desaturate by 22% and increase roughness by 0.2 — paint wears off corners first. On painted metal, reveal the substrate color underneath.

**Layer 4 — Cavity grime.** Inverse of Layer 3: in concave crevices, mortar joints, panel gaps, and corners, darken by 30% and increase roughness. Drive this from the baked AO channel.

**Layer 5 — Rust.** Only on materials flagged metallic. A domain-warped fbm mask (warp 24, freq 0.05) biased toward: contact points with other metal, fasteners, welds, bottom edges, and anywhere the water-streak mask is strong. Three-stop color ramp `#8A4A28` → `#6B3418` → `#3E2410`. Rust sets metalness to 0.05 and roughness to 0.92, and displaces the height map outward by 0.4 mm at the heaviest stage.

**Layer 6 — Biological growth.** Moss and lichen, gated by biome moisture:
- Moss `#4A6B32`: on surfaces where `worldNormal.y > 0.3` OR `dot(worldNormal, northVector) > 0.5`, in the swamp (intensity 1.0), suburbs (0.35), mountains below tree line (0.5), city (0.15). Concentrated in cavities and at base-of-wall contact with ground.
- Lichen `#9AA88C`: on exposed rock and old wood, patchy worley-shaped, mountains and swamp.
- Algae `#3E5A32`: swamp only, on anything below the waterline plus 0.4 m, plus a distinct **tide line** stain band.
- Vines: on 30% of swamp structures and 12% of city walls, as geometry (not texture) — instanced tendrils climbing 2–6 m with leaf clusters.

**Layer 7 — Human marks.** Graffiti (city ×1.0, docks ×1.2, suburbs ×0.15, elsewhere 0): 18 procedurally drawn tag shapes from a stroke library, random color from an 8-swatch palette, applied as decals to flat vertical surfaces below 3.5 m. Posters and flyers on utility poles and boarded windows. Scuff marks at door handles and stair edges. Rubber tire marks on roads at intersections and the switchback hairpins.

**Layer 8 — Damage and decay.** Per-instance: 12% of windows already broken, 8% of doors hanging open at odd angles, 6% of roof sections missing shingles revealing sheathing, 4% of walls with a hole revealing framing. Pick these deterministically from the instance seed so they're identical every load.

## O.2 Gameplay VFX detail

**Muzzle flash:** 3-layer — a bright core quad (1.5 frames), a radial star flare, and a smoke puff. Per-weapon size and color. Illuminates nearby geometry via a 1-frame point light (pooled, max 4 active).

**Bullet impacts:** per Section K.2. Additionally, impacts on any surface within 2 m of grass or foliage trigger a local disturbance in the grass shader.

**Tracers:** not every bullet. 1 in 4 rounds, as a stretched billboard 1.8 m long fading over 0.06 s, with a slight color variance. Tracer color: `#FFC46E` friendly-side, seen from behind; incoming tracers read `#FF8A4E`.

**Blood:** a directional spray of 14 particles along the bullet vector, a hit-flash on the character, and a decal on the surface behind if within 3 m. Persist 30 s.

**Explosions:** a 5-layer effect — flash, fireball, expanding smoke ring, debris shower (12 physics-enabled chunks, material-matched to the ground), and a ground scorch decal 6 m diameter. Add a screen-space shockwave distortion ring and a 0.4 s low-pass filter on all audio for anyone within 12 m.

**Smoke grenade:** 900 particles filling a 9 m sphere over 2.5 s, with turbulent internal motion, and — critically — an actual density field that the bot vision raycast samples. Visual smoke that AI sees through is worse than no smoke.

## O.3 Wet weather surface state (optional but high value)

A global `wetness` parameter 0–1 that darkens all albedo by up to 25%, reduces roughness by up to 0.45, and enables puddle accumulation in concave areas (driven by the height map's local minima). Even fixed at a low value like 0.15 in the swamp and 0 elsewhere, this makes biomes feel physically different.

# SECTION R — Verification for the Detail Pass

Report all of the following after each section:

1. **Draw call delta** and **triangle delta** at Tier 0, measured at 6 fixed camera positions: suburban lawn, city alley, city rooftop, swamp waterline, mountain forest floor, mine interior.
2. **Texture memory** total and per-material breakdown.
3. **Frame time delta** in ms for each detail system, isolated by toggling.
4. A **detail density audit**: stand at each of the 6 positions and count visible unique prop types. Target is 25+ at every position. Fewer than 15 means the space reads as empty regardless of polygon count.
5. **The 2-meter test**: at every one of the 6 positions, crouch and look at the ground within 2 m. There must be at least 4 distinct detail layers visible (terrain texture, grass, ground clutter, decals). If a surface looks flat and uniform at 2 m, it fails.
6. **The repetition test**: pan the camera 360° at each position and identify any object appearing more than 4 times in an obviously identical form. Fix by increasing per-instance variation, not by removing objects.
